---
title: MyInvestor
description: Connect MyInvestor's current account as a bank, and its funds, ETFs, and pension plans with their units.
---

MyInvestor connects in two parts, because it shares them in two different ways.

- **The current account** is a bank account, so it connects through PSD2 like any other bank. On **Banks**, pick **Spain** and then **MyInvestor Banco**, and approve the consent at MyInvestor. MyInvestor allows a consent for 90 days, so the app asks you to reconnect about every 90 days.
- **Funds, ETFs, and pension plans** aren't reachable through PSD2, and MyInvestor has no API for them. You add each one with the units you hold, and the app prices them every morning at Morningstar's latest net asset value (NAV).

Nothing signs in to MyInvestor for the funds. The list of funds and units is stored encrypted, like any other connection's credentials.

## Add your funds

::steps
### Open the connect dialog
Open **Investments**, click **MyInvestor**, and click **Connect**.

### Find each fund
Type its ISIN or its name under **Find a fund, ETF, or pension plan**, and pick it from the list. MyInvestor shows the ISIN on each fund's page. Pension plans have no ISIN, so search for them by name, for example `MyInvestor Indexado Global`.

### Enter the units
Next to each fund, type the units (participaciones) that MyInvestor's app shows for it. Both `120,53` and `120.53` work.

### Check and connect
Click **Check and connect**. The app prices every fund once before it saves anything, and then creates a **MyInvestor** investment account that holds them.
::

## When you buy or sell

MyInvestor doesn't share your orders, so the app can't see a new buy or a sale. Update the units yourself:

- Open the **MyInvestor** account, and under **Settings** type the new units of a fund and click **Update**. The account syncs at once. Units of `0` remove the fund.
- To add a fund you didn't have, open the connect dialog again and add it with the rest. Connecting again replaces the whole list and keeps the account and its history.

## What it reads

- Each fund's units, and its latest NAV from Morningstar in the fund's own currency, converted to the household's currency.
- The account's value each day from the day you connect it. The balance chart starts on that day, because MyInvestor doesn't share past positions.

It doesn't read your orders, dividends, or fees, and it can't trade or move money. Money you send to MyInvestor from another bank shows as a transfer.

::note
A NAV is published a day or two after the date it's for, so the account's value trails the market by a day or two, like MyInvestor's own valuation.
::

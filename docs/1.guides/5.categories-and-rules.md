---
title: Categories and rules
description: How transactions get a category, from rules, transfer detection, AI suggestions, and your own picks.
---

A transaction has one category, none, or explicit splits across categories. Budgets and spending charts group by category. A transaction gets its category in this order:

1. A rule whose words match it.
2. Transfer detection, when it's money moving between your own accounts.
3. An AI suggestion, when the household has AI categories turned on.
4. You, by hand. Your pick always wins, and nothing changes it afterwards.

## Categories

You manage categories in **Settings › Categories**. They belong to the household, so everyone in it shares the same list.

### Kinds

Each category has a kind. The kind decides how its transactions count.

| Kind | Shown as | How it counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `expense` | **Spending** | Money out counts as spending. Money back in, like a refund, lowers that category's spending instead of counting as income. |
| `income` | **Income** | Money in counts as income. |
| `transfer` | **Transfer** | Stays out of spending and income totals and budgets. |

### Default categories

A new household starts with these categories, named in the language of the person who created it:

- **Spending:** Groceries, Eating out, Housing, Utilities, Transport, Health, Travel, Subscriptions, Shopping, Entertainment, Fitness, Kids, Pets, Gifts, Education, Fees.
- **Income:** Salary, Other income.
- **Transfer:** Transfers.

Most of them also come with rules for common merchants and keywords. You find those rules in **Settings › Rules**, and you can change or delete them.

### Add or edit a category

::steps
### Open the form
In **Settings › Categories**, click **Add category**. To change one, click the pencil next to it.

### Fill it in
Give it a **Name**, pick its **Kind**, an **Icon**, and a **Color**.

### Save
Click **Add category**, or **Save** when you edit one.
::

### Delete a category

Click the trash icon next to a category, then **Delete category**. Its transactions become uncategorized, so rules can file them again. Its rules and budgets go with it.

## Rules

A rule files transactions by their words. You manage rules in **Settings › Rules**.

### How a rule matches

A rule matches when its words start a word in the transaction's description or payee. Case doesn't matter. For example, `fee` matches "Bank fee" but not "Coffee".

The household has one rule per set of words. Adding a rule with words that already have one updates that rule.

### Add a rule

::steps
### Enter the words
In **Settings › Rules**, type a word from the description in **Description contains**. It needs at least 2 characters.

### Pick what it does
Choose a category in **File under**. To also hide what it matches, tick **Also exclude what it matches**. Excluded transactions stay out of the transactions list and every total. You can still show them on **Transactions**.

A rule needs a category, or to exclude what it matches, or both. With exclude ticked, **File under** can be **Don't change**.

### Save
Click **Add rule**.
::

Each rule shows how many of your transactions it matches. Click the count to see them on **Transactions**. The search there finds the words anywhere in the text, so it can show a few more.

### When rules disagree

Rules have no manual order. When several rules match one transaction:

- The rule with the longest words that has a category gives the category. A rule for `uber eats` beats a rule for `uber`.
- Any matching rule that excludes excludes the transaction.

### What a rule changes

A rule only fills transactions that have no category yet. It never changes a category you picked by hand, or one that already came from another rule or from AI.

When you add a rule, it acts on your existing transactions right away:

- It files the matching ones that have no category, on accounts you can edit.
- With exclude on, it excludes the matching ones nobody decided on by hand.

On accounts you can only view, existing transactions stay as they are. New transactions on every account get the rule when they sync or when you import them.

### Change or delete a rule

- To change a rule's category, pick another one in its row. The new category goes to matching transactions that have no category.
- Use the **...** menu for **Also exclude what it matches** or **Stop excluding**, and for **Delete rule**.

Deleting a rule leaves the categories it gave. Whatever it excluded comes back, unless another rule still excludes it.

### Apply to similar

When you change a transaction's category on **Transactions**, a message asks whether to use that category for other transactions from the same payee. Click **Apply to similar** to do that.

This saves a household rule for the payee and applies it. If the transaction is on a private account, or hidden, the app saves no rule. Similar transactions on your own accounts get the category, and nobody else sees the payee.

## Rule suggestions

**Settings › Rules** also lists **Suggested rules** that it found in your transactions. Each one says how many transactions it would match and why:

| Reason | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **you filed these by hand** | You gave most of this payee's transactions the same category. |
| **AI's pick** | AI picked or guessed the same category for most of them. |
| **moves the bank makes on its own** | Round-ups, spare change, and currency exchanges. These suggest a rule that excludes them. |
| **keeps coming back, pick a category** | A payee that repeats with no category. Pick one before you add it. |

A suggestion needs at least 3 matching transactions, and the list shows up to 8 at once. Suggestions come only from shared accounts you can edit. Private accounts, hidden transactions, transfers, and money between household members never suggest a rule.

Click **Add rule** to turn a suggestion into a rule. You can change its category first. Click the **x** to dismiss it for the whole household.

## AI-suggested categories

When no rule matches a transaction, an AI model can suggest a category for it. The app uses Jev, a model from TypeSafe.

### When it runs

AI categories run only when the household has a key, either its own or the server's, and the setting is on. They never run in the demo. They run:

- After each sync, once rules and transfer detection are done, for up to 400 transactions.
- After a CSV import.
- When you click **Categorize with AI** on **Transactions**, or **Categorize uncategorized with AI** in **Settings › Categories**.

They only look at transactions that have no category, that you didn't set by hand, that aren't transfers or excluded, and that AI hasn't looked at before. Spending picks from your spending categories, and money in from your income categories. A direction needs at least two categories to choose from.

### When AI sets a category

AI sets a category only when it's sure enough: a confidence of at least 0.7, and at least 0.7 that the transaction fits one of your categories at all. Otherwise the transaction stays uncategorized. AI asks about each transaction once, so an unsure one isn't asked again.

### How suggested rows look

- A category set by AI shows a sparkles icon next to its name. Its tooltip says "Suggested by AI. Change it if it's wrong."
- In the transaction's details, it says "Suggested by AI from the description."
- For a transaction AI wasn't sure about, the details show "AI's guess, not sure enough to set it:" and a button with the guess, like **Use Groceries**. Click it to use that category.

### Override a suggestion

Pick another category in the transaction's row or details. Your pick counts as a hand-made change, so rules and AI leave it alone from then on.

### What AI sees

AI gets the description and payee, whether money went in or out, the amount rounded to two significant digits, the currency, the account type, and the household's category names and rule keywords. The app masks names of household members, IBANs, and long numbers like card and account numbers first. It never gets dates, balances, account names, or who you are.

### Settings

- The household's owner turns AI categories on or off with **Suggest categories with AI** on the **Household** page. The switch only shows when there's a key.
- The owner picks how the household reaches the AI in **Settings › Categories**, under **AI categories**: the server's own key, or the household's key for TypeSafe, Vercel AI Gateway, or Cloudflare AI Gateway. **Test** checks the setup without saving it.

## Transfers between your own accounts

Money moving between the household's accounts is neither spending nor income. The app finds these moves and gives them the transfer category, so they leave spending, income, and budgets.

### How transfers are found

The app pairs a payment out of one account with money into another account of the household when:

- The amounts match in the same currency, or are within 5% of each other once both are converted to the household's currency.
- They're at most 4 days apart.
- At least one side reads like a transfer.

A transaction reads like a transfer when its text names a currency exchange, a top-up, a pocket or vault, a transfer to your own account, a credit card payment, or a deposit at a broker or crypto exchange. A payment that names the account owner's full name counts too. Such a transaction gets the transfer category even without a pair.

Detection runs after every sync and import, and once a day. It never changes a category you picked by hand. A private account or a hidden transaction only pairs with accounts of the same owner.

### Fix a wrong transfer

Open the transaction and pick another category. If it was part of a pair, the two stop being a pair. The other side keeps the transfer category until you change it too.

If you delete every transfer category, the app creates a new **Transfers** category the next time it finds a transfer.

### Where transfers show

On **Budgets**, money moved between your accounts appears as "moved between accounts, not counted". Click it to see those transactions.

## Review transactions

Open **Transactions** and select **Needs review** to work through new activity. Categorizing and reviewing are separate: a rule or AI suggestion does not confirm that someone has checked a transaction. The details show who reviewed it and when. You can mark it unreviewed again.

In a household, assign a transaction to someone who can see its account. Hidden transactions stay with their owner. **Assigned to me** shows your open assignments. The overview keeps a setup checklist and monthly check-in available after connecting your first account.

Select rows with their checkboxes to categorize, exclude, assign, or review several at once. **Undo** restores the previous values for the same person during its short availability window. A later edit prevents an old Undo from overwriting it.

### Split a payment

Open an expense and use the split editor to allocate its amount to categories. The parts must add up to the original bank amount. Splits change category reporting without creating new bank transactions or changing the original total, date, or import identity. Remove the split to use one category again.

### Pair a refund or reimbursement

Open an incoming refund and choose its original expense. The original must be visible, in the same currency, and large enough to cover the refund. The refund reduces the expense category's spending on the date the refund actually arrived. It does not rewrite the original purchase month. Unlink it to correct the pairing. Linked refunds also appear in the original expense's details.

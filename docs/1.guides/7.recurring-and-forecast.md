---
title: Recurring and forecast
description: How the app finds subscriptions, bills, and regular income, and how it projects your balance from them.
---

The app looks for payments that repeat in your transactions. **Recurring** lists them. **Forecast** uses them, with your usual spending, to project your balance.

## Recurring payments

### How detection works

Detection looks at each account on its own, over the last 400 days. It groups transactions by merchant and by direction, money out or money in. The merchant is the payee when the bank names one. Otherwise it's the first words of the description, without dates, card numbers, references, and payment words like "card payment".

A group becomes a recurring payment when the usual gap between its payments fits a cadence:

| Cadence | Usual gap | Payments needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Weekly** | 5 to 9 days | 3 |
| **Monthly** | 26 to 35 days | 3 |
| **Every 3 months** | 85 to 98 days | 3 |
| **Yearly** | 350 to 380 days | 2 |

It also needs all of these:

- At least two thirds of the gaps fit the cadence.
- The amounts stay within 25% of their usual amount, or at least 80% of them are the same.
- The last payment isn't too old: no more than two periods and 5 days ago. A cancelled subscription drops out this way.

Two payments on the same day count as one. Excluded transactions and hidden transactions never make a recurring payment.

Detection runs after every sync and CSV import. It also runs when someone excludes or hides a transaction.

> [!NOTE]
> A new account needs a few months of transactions first. Subscriptions and bills show up once an account has two or three months of history.

### Confirm or dismiss

New recurring payments show a **Detected** badge. For each one:

- Click the check (**Confirm it repeats**) to confirm it.
- Click the **x** (**Not recurring**) to dismiss it.

With more than one detected payment, **Confirm all** confirms them all at once. For a confirmed payment, **Not recurring** is in its **...** menu.

You need edit access to the account to confirm or dismiss its payments.

| Status | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Detected | Counts in the totals and the forecast. Updates with every detection. |
| Confirmed | Counts in the totals and the forecast. Stays even if detection stops finding it, and its next date keeps moving forward. The forecast is surer of it. |
| Dismissed | Stays out of the totals and the forecast. |

Dismissed payments are under **Not recurring** at the bottom of the page. Click **Restore** to bring one back.

A confirmed payment still goes away if detection stops finding it because some of its payments were hidden.

### What the page shows

- **Charges a month**, **Charges a year**, and **Income a month**. Totals of the payments that aren't dismissed, in the household's currency. A weekly payment counts 52 times a year, a monthly one 12, and so on.
- **Upcoming charges**, grouped by when they're next due: **Overdue**, **Next 7 days**, **Later this month**, then by month. Switch to **Income** to see regular income.
- A payment whose price changed shows what it **was** before.

### Ways to save

Above the list, the page can point out ways to save:

| Insight | When it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Price went up** | A fixed-price charge went up by 3% or more in the last 180 days. It needs at least two payments at the old price. |
| **Paid twice** | Two or more people in the household pay for the same service. |
| **Large subscription** | A payment in the household's Subscriptions category costs at least 30 EUR a month, or the same in your currency. |

**Alerts** can also tell you when a subscription or bill costs more than before.

## Forecast

**Forecast** projects your cash day by day, from today's balance. Use the **How far ahead** control to pick 3, 6, or 12 months. Six is the default.

### What it starts from

The forecast covers accounts you can spend from: **Checking**, **Savings**, **Cash**, and **Credit card** accounts. It leaves out archived accounts and accounts that aren't in net worth.

It starts from their balances today, added up in the household's currency.

### How it's built

Each day, the expected balance changes by:

- **Recurring payments**, on the days they're due. Detected and confirmed payments count. Each one uses its latest amount if the price is fixed, or its recent typical amount if it varies. A payment up to a week late still counts, today.
- **Usual spending**. Spending that no recurring payment explains, averaged over the last 3 full months and spread evenly over each day.
- **Usual moves**. Money you moved to accounts outside the forecast, averaged the same way. Moves between two accounts in the forecast cancel out.

If your data starts inside those 3 months, the average starts at your oldest transaction. With under 4 weeks of data, it uses every day from the oldest transaction to yesterday.

The forecast runs to the end of the last month you picked. The current month counts as the first.

### What it ignores

- Accounts of other kinds, like investments, crypto, and loans.
- Dismissed recurring payments.
- Excluded transactions.
- Income that doesn't repeat. The forecast only projects recurring income.
- Other people's private accounts and hidden transactions.

> [!TIP]
> If the forecast only goes down, it hasn't found regular income yet. A salary shows up after three payments.

### The estimated range

The shaded range illustrates variation observed in your history. It is not a calibrated probability or a guarantee. It comes from how much your spending moved from week to week, over up to the last 26 weeks. It widens the further ahead you look, and it's wider for payments whose amounts vary.

With under three weeks of transactions, the range is a guess. With under 60 days, the page says the forecast is a rough first look.

Each upcoming payment also shows how sure the forecast is of it: **Sure**, **Likely**, or **Unsure**. That depends on how regular its dates and amounts have been, and how many payments it has.

### What the page shows

- A summary with **Cash today**, the **Lowest point**, and the **Next 30 days**.
- The balance chart: **Actual** until today, then **Expected**, the **Likely range**, the lowest point, and payments in and out. **Show as table** lists it week by week.
- **Coming up**. The expected payments, money **Coming in** and **Going out**. Pick a day to see only its payments.
- **Month by month**. Each month's **Income**, **Bills**, **Usual spending**, **Moved out**, what's **Left over**, and **Balance at the end**.
- Each account on its own, when the forecast covers more than one.

### What if

The **What if** panel reruns the forecast in your browser. The working scenario stays on this device when you navigate away or reload, separately for each person, household, and scope.

- **Can we afford it?** Choose the paying account, enter a **One-off amount** and a date in **On**. Set a minimum reserve and choose whether to protect active goal allocations. The answer checks the paying account, other cash-account shortfalls, and a conservative cash-only range. Credit-card debt remains in the net forecast; card affordability stays unknown because the app does not track credit limits. Private cash in another account does not make the paying account safe.
- **Income**. Move the slider to change every regular income by -50% to +30%.
- **Counted payments**. Untick a payment to see the forecast without it, like a subscription you cancel or an income that stops.

- **Spending source** uses historical spending or repeats your current budgets each month. The budget option leaves recurring bills counted once and retains usual unbudgeted spending. Future scheduled budget changes are not applied to this assumption.
- **Stress tests** delay regular income by a week or increase everyday spending by 15%.
- **Planned events** add dated income or expenses to an account, once or on a weekly, monthly, quarterly, or yearly schedule. An end date stops repetitions. Use a skipped recurring bill plus a replacement planned event for a rent change.

The chart, monthly totals, calendar, and individual account projections all follow the same scenario. A planned purchase appears on its date. A what-if moves the expected balance, but not the width of the range. Click **Reset** to go back.

### Low balance alerts

**Alerts** warns you when the forecast goes under an amount within the next 30 days. You set the amount in **Alert settings**, under **When the forecast goes under**.

### Save, share, and compare plans

Name and save a plan to your account to keep it across devices. Duplicate a plan to try an alternative, select a saved plan to resume it, or compare it with your working scenario on the chart. Only its owner can update or delete a saved plan.

A household plan can reference only shared accounts. The app rechecks account visibility when reading saved plans, so making an account private hides plans that reference it from other members. Deleting an account removes saved plans that reference it or its recurring payments. When someone leaves a household, their plans move privately only if all referenced accounts move with them. Plans that cross the household boundary are removed. Plans change no bank transactions. In the public demo, working scenarios stay on your device; saving or sharing to the server is disabled.

### Check the inputs before spending

**Assumptions and sources** lists included accounts, balance dates, last syncs, and exchange-rate quality. A missing currency rate can leave an approximate total. Missing or stale inputs prevent a positive affordability answer. Short histories need checking too. Historical chart values may be reconstructed from known balances and valued at current exchange rates.

Goals protect their currently funded allocations. Future monthly savings targets are guidance, not bank transfers automatically added to the projection.

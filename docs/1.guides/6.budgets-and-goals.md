---
title: Budgets and goals
description: Set a monthly limit per category, and track what you're saving for.
---

Budgets cap what you spend in a category each month. Goals track money you're putting aside for something.

## Budgets

A budget is a monthly spending limit for one spending category. It starts over each month unless you enable **Carry unused budget**. You find budgets on **Budgets**.

### Add a budget

::steps
### Open the form
On **Budgets**, click **Add budget**. With no budgets yet, click **Add a budget**.

### Pick the category and amount
Choose a **Category**. The list only has spending categories. Enter the amount **Per month**, in the household's currency.

### Choose who it's for
In a household with more than one person, **Just for me** makes it a personal budget. Leave it off for a household budget.

### Save
Click **Add budget**.
::

Each category can have one household budget, and each person one personal budget for it.

### Household and personal budgets

| | Household budget | Personal budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counts spending on | Every account you can see | Only your own accounts |
| Who sees it | Everyone in the household | Only you |

The page lists them as **Household budgets** and **Your personal budgets**. To add your first personal budget, click **Add a personal budget**.

### How spending counts

A budget counts the money that left your accounts in its category during the month:

- Refunds into the category lower its spending.
- Excluded transactions don't count.
- Transfers between your accounts don't count, since they have the transfer category.
- The app converts amounts in other currencies to the household's currency.

The top of the page shows **Spent of budgeted** for the month, and how much is left or over. When you have household budgets, this total counts only those, since personal budgets can cover the same spending.

**Outside any budget** is spending in categories without a budget, plus uncategorized spending. Next to it, **moved between accounts, not counted** shows the transfers left out. Click it to see them.

### Suggestions and dated changes

When completed months of transactions are available, **Use this average** fills in a suggested amount. The form shows which months were used. You choose whether to accept it.

**Applies from** chooses this month or a future month. Changes preserve earlier monthly amounts. Limits changed before this feature was introduced cannot be reconstructed; those older months use the previously stored amount. A scheduled future change remains in place until you edit it. To change a budget's category or sharing, create a new budget so its history stays consistent.

### Carry unused budget

Rollover is off by default. Enabling **Carry unused budget** starts with zero carry in its effective month. Each following month adds the unused balance of the previous month. Overspending does not create negative carry. Turning rollover off resets it.

For example, a monthly limit of €400 with €350 spent adds €50 to the next month's available budget. A changed transaction can change the carry. Future carry is an estimate based on spending recorded so far.

### Reading a budget

Each budget shows spending against the available amount, including any carry. This month also shows elapsed-month pacing and a projection at your current spending rate. Bills paid early can make that projection uneven. The bar:

- The bar turns amber at 85% of the budget.
- It turns red past 100%.
- The line under it says how much is left or over, and the percentage.

Use the arrows next to the month to see past months, and **This month** to come back.

### Budget alerts

**Alerts** tells you when a budget reaches 90% of its available amount, and again when it goes over. To change the percentage or turn this off, open **Alert settings** and change **When a budget reaches**. These settings are yours: others in the household keep their own.

### Edit or delete a budget

Click a budget's name to edit it. The **...** menu has **Edit budget** and **Delete budget**. Deleting a budget keeps its transactions. Only the monthly limit goes.

Deleting a category also deletes its budgets.

## Goals

A goal is something you're saving for, like an emergency fund or a trip. You find goals on **Goals**.

### Create a goal

::steps
### Open the form
On **Goals**, click **New goal**. With no goals yet, click **Create a goal**.

### Name it
Give it a **Name** and pick an **Icon**.

### Set the target
Enter the **Target** amount, in the household's currency. Optionally, pick a date in **By**. The date must be today or later.

### Choose how to track progress
Under **Track progress**, pick one:
- **Linked account**. Choose the backing account and enter **Allocated to this goal**. Linking does not allocate the account's whole balance.
- **By hand**. Enter what you've put aside in **Saved so far**, and update it when you add more.

### Choose who sees it
In a household with more than one person, **Just for me** makes it a personal goal that only you see. Leave it off to share it with everyone.

### Save
Click **Create goal**.
::

### How progress works

An allocation reserves money you already have, in the household's currency. It does not move money at your bank. Several goals can use the same account, but their combined allocations cannot exceed its available cash.

Personal goals use your private accounts. Shared goals use shared accounts. This prevents private allocations from being revealed through a household's remaining cash. You need edit access to the backing account. If an account's sharing changes, review the goal's allocation again. A private backing account's allocation is hidden from other members.

If the account balance drops below its allocations, funded progress is reduced proportionally across active goals. The requested allocations remain saved and each goal shows its shortfall. For example, two €600 allocations backed by €1,000 of cash have €500 funded progress each.

Existing linked goals ask you to review their allocations. Their previously entered savings are preserved; the migration does not assign the entire account balance to every goal.

Archived accounts aren't offered for linking.

### What a goal shows

Each goal shows a progress ring, what's saved of the target, and two lines:

- **Each month**. How much to put aside each month to reach the target by its date. It divides what's left by the whole months until the date. Without a date, it says **Set a date to see it**. It says **Reached** once you have the target, and **Date passed** when the date is behind you and the goal isn't reached.
- **Cash reserved in**. The backing account's name, or **Updated by hand** for manual savings.

In a shared household, a badge says whether the goal is **Shared** or **Personal**.

The top of the page adds up what's funded across active goals, and how much a month keeps every goal on time.

### Edit or delete a goal

Click a goal's name to edit it. The **...** menu has **Edit goal** and **Delete goal**. Deleting a goal leaves your accounts and their money as they are.

### Complete or reactivate a goal

Choose **Complete goal and release reservation** from its menu when it is finished. It stays visible for reference but stops reserving cash and leaves the active totals. **Reactivate goal** checks that the account can fund its allocation again.
